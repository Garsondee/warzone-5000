# History

## Timeline

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1999 | Pumpkin Studios develops Warzone 2100; Eidos Interactive publishes it for PC (Windows) and PlayStation. |
| Oct 1999 | Fan group N.E.W.S.T. begins trying to keep the game supported. |
| Nov 1999 | Official patch 1.10 (final). |
| 5 Jan 2000 | Pumpkin stops supporting the game. |
| Mar 2000 | Pumpkin Studios closed (Wikipedia says 15 Mar; the studio's own farewell says 13 Mar, so sources disagree). Cause: Eidos cancelled another Pumpkin project, *Saboteur*, removing funding. Staff later regrouped as **Pivotal Games**. |
| Nov 2000 | N.E.W.S.T. starts releasing unofficial patches. |
| 6 Dec 2004 | Eidos releases source and most data under **GPL-2.0-or-later**. (One source says 7 Dec; the project's founding is listed as 4 Dec.) Credit is given to the fan community "Pumpkin-2" for persuading the rights holders. |
| 2004+ | "Warzone 2100 Resurrection Project" forms, later renamed "Warzone 2100 Project". |
| 10 Jun 2008 | Remaining data (e.g. music/videos) released openly. |
| Feb 2008 | Resurrection Project 1.2.0 beta announced (per a GameWatcher news item; version lineage between 2008 and 4.0 not researched). |
| 2021 | Version 4.0: Vulkan, factions, big engine modernisation. |
| 2026 | Version 4.7 (April). |

## What the community has added since 2004
Higher resolution textures, new shaders, support for additional platforms (Linux, macOS, later BSD and web), a
JavaScript scripting API, new AI bots, improved networking and lobby, balance work, translations (Gettext/Crowdin),
and campaign mods.

## Why this matters for us (principles)
- **Open-sourcing preserved the game.** The studio died within months of release, yet the game is still being
  actively developed 25+ years later. Games with moddable, inspectable data tend to outlive their makers.
- **Community maintenance changes the product.** Many "features" players love today (factions, spectators, rated
  duels) did not exist in 1999. The original is a foundation, not the finished form.
