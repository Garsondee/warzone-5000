# Track animation and the sinkage overlay: plan (slice 2 stage B, text only, no code until the contact data is real)

*VIEWER, 2026-10-10. Drafted at ARCH's request after GEOMETRY's carrier (#134), TRACKS's gear (#120, #125) and contract 0.3.0 (0982843) landed. Nothing here is built; the first PR starts when ARCH says the contact data is real.*

## What I am given (read from the code)
- **The belt needs no new replay data.** `RenderRig::track_runs` (contract 0.3.0) lists, per belt: the wheels it wraps in loop order with their path radius, `link_mesh` (one link at the origin, +X along travel), the design link count `links`, the sprocket (`sprocket`, `sprocket_joint`) and `direction`. The sprocket's spin is already one of the frame's joint coordinates, so my codec carries it today (smooth, delta-coded like any wheel). The belt moves exactly as the simulated sprocket turns.
- **GEOMETRY has the path in closed form** (`w5k_geo::track`): `Belt::round(circles)` is the taut band round the wheel circles (tangent lines and arcs, length `L`), `tile(L, p)` gives `n = round(L / p)` links at pitch `L / n`, `belt_mesh(.., phase)` places them. For the placeholder carrier: pitch 0.152 m, belt 0.381 m wide, 11 sprocket teeth (pitch radius about 0.27 m). Its own note calls this "the motion path of an animation package": instances spread by arc length along a closed curve, each oriented by the tangent. That is exactly what I draw.
- **Nothing moves yet.** GEOMETRY and FORGE still emit a static belt mesh per side and an empty `track_runs`; `box_tank()` has no runs either (a test builds one). So the first thing I need is not data but a rig: I will assemble a canned tracked rig with runs inside `w5k viewer dump-canned` (public contract types, my own file), so nothing waits on other lanes.
- **The ground data already has a place.** `ContactFrame { flags, normal_force_n, sinkage_m, slip, material }` per contact, names in the header; TRACKS reports per-sample outputs (12 cells a side on the reference tank, each with its `sinkage_m`, from the series spring "wheel contact in series with the soil").

## The belt
Per frame and per run: take each wheel's node position in the `Track` node's frame (the suspension moves them), build the band round those circles (a port of `Belt::round`, about 60 lines), put link `i` at arc length `s_i = (i L / n + phase) mod L` with `phase = direction x sprocket spin x pitch radius`, orient it by the tangent, and write the matrices of one `InstancedMesh` per run (about 100 links a side: negligible). `L` changes as the suspension moves; `n` does not, so the spacing is `L / n`, as the contract says.
**Rubber band round nails.** A road wheel lifted by a bump sits inside the band and the belt does not touch it, so the viewer *skips* a wheel the band does not touch instead of failing (GEOMETRY refuses such a wheel at design time, correctly, because there it is a rig error; at run time it is just a bump). A wheel pushed down by rebound extends the band and is wrapped.

## The risk that decides the design: the belt aliases at ordinary speeds
Links 0.152 m apart advance `v / f` per displayed frame. At 30 frames a second the belt looks frozen at 4.6 m/s (16 km/h) and runs *backwards* anywhere between half a pitch and a pitch of advance per frame (2.3 to 4.6 m/s); at 60 Hz the frozen speed is 9.1 m/s (33 km/h). That is the wagon-wheel effect of `docs/theory/viewer.md` again, now on the speeds a tank spends its life at. The data is right; the sampling is too coarse.
**Fix: temporal supersampling, i.e. motion blur.** Draw each displayed frame as `K` sub-frame phases spread over a shutter angle (180 degrees, half a frame, is the film default) and blend them: instanced links at `K` phases with alpha `1/K` live, `K` accumulated sub-frames in the recorder (`K = 8` is 800 instances a side; the cost is the fragment work, and only when a belt is on screen). Rejected: fading to a plain band above a threshold speed (cheap, but the belt pops and stops showing links, which is what the owner wants to see). The same trick would cure the wheel lugs and the sprocket, so it goes in as a shared helper.

## The overlay (sinkage now, pressure when the footprint is known)
1. **A strip under each ground run**, one cell per TRACKS sample, coloured by `sinkage_m` on a single-hue sequential ramp (palette validated as in `charts.md`) with a legend in millimetres.
2. **A ribbon trail** behind each track and wheel for the last N seconds, shaded by the sinkage of the cell that passed. It is a record of measured values, not a soil memory (TRACKS lists persistent ruts as a non-goal), and the legend says so.
3. **Ground pressure** is `normal_force_n / (b dx)`, and neither the belt width `b` nor the cell length `dx` is in `RenderRig` or the header. **Request (ARCH or TRACKS), default: show sinkage and force only**, and the pressure legend appears when the header carries `contact_area_m2` per contact name (additive, defaulted). Wheels use the same overlay with their own `sinkage_m`, so the trucks in the mud pit leave ruts too.
Bytes: a track adds 12 contacts a side; contacts are delta-coded, so I expect a tracked carrier to land near the 100 B per vehicle-frame ceiling (a minute of driving, about 180 KB). I will measure on real data, not guess.

## Theory for the owner (to move into `docs/theory/viewer.md` when built)
A taut belt is a rubber band stretched round nails: it touches only the nails on the outside of its convex hull, so a wheel lifted inside the hull simply stops touching it. Animating links along it is instancing along a curve by arc length (a "motion path"), and the speed along the curve is the sprocket's rolling distance. A film camera at 24 frames a second with a 180 degree shutter blurs fast motion over half a frame, which is exactly the low-pass filter a sampled image needs; the belt looks right for the same reason a spinning wheel in a film does.
